# Arrival recap for Google Flow

Place this sequence immediately after the existing opening cinematics, where the three interactive story-map beats currently play. Give the editor three separate silent 16:9 clips at 1920×1080, about 6 seconds each, with a steady second at either end for trimming to the recorded voice. Keep the recap playable with stills and captions. These are production prompts. The game currently uses its own recap until these renders are delivered.

Use the last frame of the supplied descent clip and the existing arrival-bay artwork as visual references. Match the silhouette of the actual capsule, suit, doors, consoles and workbench. Keep identifiers and any text out of the generated image. Add captions, the project's AI-video indicator and recorded speech in the edit. The story must make sense with sound off. The same small white capsule, blue emergency beacon and orange handrail appear in all three clips.

**01 • Separation** — Generate a single readable shot in the established NEILO family adventure style, tactile materials and soft lighting. A crew ship travels above a clearly recognizable rust-colored Mars. At its side sits one attached safety capsule. Hold a wide view showing both craft. A coupling flashes amber and releases. The capsule gently separates and descends toward Mars as the large crew ship stays high and steady. End on both distinct silhouettes in the same frame. Gentle lateral camera motion, consistent scale and geography. A technical fault caused the unplanned separation. Keep the child safe, without explosions, antagonists or a heroic launch gesture. No music, voices, subtitles, badges or generated text.

Voice cue — “A fault separated your safety capsule from the crew’s ship.”

**02 • Two places** — Match the capsule from shot one. Open on the capsule settling beside a quiet, enclosed Mars research base, with the landing gear touching dusty ground. A soft puff of dust settles. Frame a helmeted explorer from behind looking up through the capsule glass. Dissolve to a simple cinematic cutaway showing the base at the surface and the crew ship still orbiting far above it. Keep the crew ship distinct from the capsule. Make the distance legible for a seven-year-old. Avoid adding another arriving spaceship, a populated colony, text, logos, dialogue or music.

Voice cue — “Your crew is still in orbit. Your capsule landed at the old base.”

**03 • First goal** — From the child’s eye level, move slowly through the airlock toward the same workbench from the playable arrival bay. A small emergency fan turns and an amber heater glows. Beside them the dark radio has an unlit signal meter and a still antenna. The explorer’s gloved hand presses its call control once. The meter stays dark. A thin blue guide light comes on along the floor toward the workbench. Let the camera settle at playable eye level facing that light. Keep the rest of the base dim and dusty. No speaking robot, working radio, outdoor walk, captions or music.

Voice cue — “Emergency air and heat are on. The radio is off. Find a way to reach your crew.”

For the final cut, use the approved recorded line for each beat as the timing reference. If the wording changes, synchronize the caption and edit to the final audio. The player should be able to point to the crew, point to the child's location, and recognize the radio problem before taking the first step.
