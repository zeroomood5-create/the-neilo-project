Plants need suitable light, water, nutrients, temperature and air. Our two switches represent only part of that environment.


CSA lists about 21–28 days for leaf lettuce and 45–65 days for soybeans. Our planner used the upper end of each range. Real growing conditions can change the result.


The example soybeans need boiling. Lettuce can be eaten without cooking. Crops add different nutrients to meals, alongside stored food.


RAD aboard Curiosity measured radiation on Mars. Our archive shows 84 absorbed dose rate readings, in micrograys per hour, from 1 January 2015.


In a separate 2016 study at Butte M12, surrounding terrain blocked part of the sky. Researchers found a 4% decrease in expected dose rate there.


A missing value means that measurement is unavailable. A reading at one place does not tell us whether another site is safe.


Solar panels turn sunlight into electricity. Some spacecraft use sunlight to run their equipment.


A battery stores energy. Spacecraft can use that stored energy when sunlight is unavailable.


An electric motor transfers energy into motion. In our base, the pump uses part of the battery reserve.


On Hubble, electricity powers instruments, computers, heaters and transmitters.


Running more devices at once asks for more power. In our base, the pump and radio together exceed the supply limit.


Pausing one job made room for the next. The battery reserve showed how much stored energy remained.
