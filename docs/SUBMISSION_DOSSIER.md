# NEILO — First Signal

Project record for the Junior Astronaut Mission Trainer challenge, prepared 14 November 2026, Cairo. Observation dates below identify when a rover collected information. Release history and media records separately identify implementation and production dates.

## Project details

A child names an explorer, chooses a full-body suit and lands beside a dormant Mars research base after a capsule fault separates them from their crew. The child finds NEILO, wires up a pump, prepares water, restores radio contact, grows food and inspects rover evidence. Emergency life support stays on while the child brings other equipment back online. The demo follows one chapter inside the base. Exploring the surface together is the proposed next chapter.

The design targets ages 7–12. Instructions accompany an immediate action. For example, a child can remove a plug from a working circuit and see the device lose power, or identify exactly which plant remains to be harvested. Science recaps revisit what just happened. The player can ask for a hint and try again. Browser progress saves allow a child to return to an unfinished task. The educational aim is to help children connect their own decisions with an explanation of the result. Learning outcomes have yet to be measured in a child study.

The public static build offers prepared explorer art. A separate development proxy can generate a suit from a portrait with a grown-up’s consent and a configured service key. The game can be played using a nickname and a preset suit. The gameplay video shows a fictional demo name and a preset.

## Scientific material used in play

| Where the player meets it | Evidence and interpretation | Values introduced by game design |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pump and water unit | Solar panels turn sunlight into electrical energy. A battery supplies stored energy, including during shadow. Hubble distributes electrical power to equipment including heaters and transmitters. An electric motor drives the illustrated pump. [NASA Space Place](https://spaceplace.nasa.gov/what-powers-a-spacecraft/en/) · [Hubble electrical system, Overview/Batteries](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/hubble/observatory/design/electrical-power/) | Base capacity, plug behavior, equipment costs, water-pack yield and preparation cycles keep the trade-off playable. The water unit represents moving/preparing stored water. |
| Radio | Real Mars rovers can send information up to spacecraft in orbit. NEILO's story uses the same surface-to-orbit relationship to reach a fictional nearby crew. [NASA Mars Relay Network, How It Works](https://science.nasa.gov/mars/mars-relay-network/) | Crew, console settings, response timing, alignment, reserves and required status message belong to the story. |
| Garden | CSA's table gives green leaf lettuce about 21–28 days with no processing and soybean about 45–65 days with boiling in the shell. ESA describes the roles of light, water, air, nutrients and suitable temperature. [CSA potential crops table](https://www.asc-csa.gc.ca/eng/youth-educators/activities/fitness-and-nutrition/interplanetary-farming.asp) · [ESA Astrofarmer PR42, pp. 5–7](https://climatedetectives.esa.int/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/PR42_AstroFarmer-1.pdf) | Inside a controlled fictional habitat, the player handles light and water. The model chooses day 28 or 65, speeds up time, and assigns meal targets at day 35 or 70. Each tray has three harvestable plants. The CSA examples are approximate and actual conditions affect yields and timing. |
| Field notes, archived instrument plot | The 84 time-stamped records are real Mars-surface absorbed dose rates from the Curiosity Radiation Assessment Detector on 1 January 2015, in µGy/h. Every equivalent-dose value in this sample is missing. [RadLab API and field definitions](https://visualization.osdr.nasa.gov/radlab/gui/data-api/) · [exact request and original retrieval time](../data/review-2026-11-13/rad-provenance.json) · [shipped records](../dist/data/rad-2015-01-01.json) | Scouting turns and the report's location choices are fictional. An unmeasured site remains unknown. |
| Field notes, September 2016 comparison | During the separate stop beside Murray Buttes M12 in September 2016, the researchers found a reduction of about 4% in the expected dose rate. JPL also reports a 7.5% decrease in neutral particle radiation. These percentages refer to that location and observation period. [Ehresmann et al., JGR Planets (2021), DOI 10.1029/2021JE006851](https://doi.org/10.1029/2021JE006851) · [JPL research account](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/how-nasas-curiosity-rover-is-making-mars-safer-for-astronauts/) | The 2015 series and the 2016 comparison support different parts of the report. Neither establishes a safe human exposure threshold or predicts conditions inside the fictional base. |
| Arrival-bay window | Curiosity’s Mastcam photographed layered sandstone at Murray Buttes on sol 1454, 8 September 2016. JPL interprets the layers as the record of wind-deposited sand. The window opens a rover archive view. [JPL image PIA21044](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/images/pia21044-farewell-to-murray-buttes-image-4/) | The base is fictional. The photo remains the original rover image. It has no added buildings, characters or AI imagery. |

Public play uses the packaged archive, with no NASA API key or live data connection required. The original screening documents preserve proposed uses and their limits. ## Verification and media provenance

On 14 November the exact recorded RadLab GET request returned 84 data rows. Comparing all returned rows and fields with the playable JSON found an exact match. The absorbed values range from 5.064985 to 7.3034263 µGy/h. The equivalent-dose field is null 84 times. The packaged file and preserved original have SHA-256 `96535699e052b21e13d8fcbaa5b8f5a81e871f2ed13b434498295b8e39876a2`. The archived API uses the column order in its `columns` field. A single-day sample cannot describe an annual or lifetime dose.

The window photograph is JPL's original 1344 × 1200 JPEG, 225,551 bytes. SHA-256 `0ba1cea422c22e393790d2ce7de28c3e79a34a18d8d80a5128a4b2717b6c0955`. Published 9 September 2016. Credit **NASA/JPL-Caltech/MSSS** appears under the image in game. This photo predates the separate M12 stop and provides regional visual context. [Original JPEG](https://d2pn8kiwq2w21t.cloudfront.net/original_images/jpegPIA21044.jpg) · [JPL use policy](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/jpl-image-use-policy/)

Source checks compare the shipped content with provider descriptions and data. The science cards retain their human-review status in the project records. Software playthroughs measure whether actions and progress work. The informal first-player test identified confusion at arrival, navigation and mission order. Those findings led the team to add a story recap, a guided first action and task dependencies.
